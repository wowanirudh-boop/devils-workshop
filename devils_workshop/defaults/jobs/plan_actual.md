<!--
Built-in instruction for the planner's look at a chapter as it was actually written, before the next
chapter's briefs are written from it. Edit it on the Prompts screen or under Story files → Prompts.
-->
The chapter in <chapter_text> has been written from the briefs in <briefs>. The writer doesn't always
follow a brief exactly, and the next chapter must follow on from what was actually written, not from
what was planned.

Read the chapter's text and write how things actually stand at its end, for planning what follows
(the writer never sees it): where every character is, what each knows, feels and wants now, what they
carry, injuries, the time and weather, and every open thread. Carry forward everything in
<continuity_before> that still holds. Then list where the text departs from the briefs in a way that
matters later (a new fact, a name, something revealed early, something left out); none is fine.

Reply exactly like this:
<continuity>
How things stand at the end of this chapter, as a compact list.
</continuity>
<differences>
- one line for each departure that matters, or nothing
</differences>
