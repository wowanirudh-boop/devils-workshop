Built-in instruction for bringing in a plan the author made elsewhere (with another tool). Edit it on the
Prompts screen (for every story) or under Story files → Prompts (for one story).
-->
The author made this story's plan elsewhere, with another tool, and gives it to you in <your_plan>: its files,
in full. It is their plan. Carry it into this story's plan, faithfully, in the sections below, so the chapters,
the scenes and the writer can work from it.

What their plan decides stays as it decides it: what happens, to whom, in what order and why; the characters,
their relationships and arcs; the places and the time; the chapters, their order and what each one holds; the
ending. Carry all of it, with its detail (the lines it gives people to say, the objects, the settings), into the
right sections. Never add, remove, merge, move or reorder an event, a character or a chapter: where it divides
the story into chapters, those are the chapters ("Chapters (proposed)"), one for one, in its order.

How it is shown is decided by the author's instructions (at the end of this instruction, and in
<writer_instructions>), not by their plan, which may come from a tool that holds content back. Where the author's
instructions want on the page, fully and in direct words, what the plan keeps vague, summarises, softens, puts in
polite words or leaves off the page (a fade-out, a line like "things go further", a moment skipped over), plan
that same event as the author wants it shown: named directly, developed, given the room and the staging it
needs, in the chapter where the plan has it. Drop the plan's own instructions about holding content back where
the author's instructions say otherwise, and keep the rest of its voice and style. Where the author's
instructions want something carried through every chapter (a tone, a focus, a charge), plan it into every
chapter, without adding events.

Fill only what their plan leaves out and the story needs (a length, a point of view, a minor name), with your
best choice, marked (my choice). Put on the agenda only what is genuinely open and important to the book.

Reply with these tags only: an <edit section="..."> with the complete text of every section (they are all new),
a <decision> for each choice their plan makes that shapes the book, the <agenda>, <ready/> if nothing
important is left open, and a <reply>:
<edit section="Main characters">
...the whole text of that section, in Markdown, without its heading...
</edit>
<decision>One line: something their plan settles</decision>
<agenda>
- **The length:** what the author must decide, in one line
</agenda>
<ready/>
<reply>
A few lines to the author: that you carried their plan in; what you show more fully or more directly than it
did, chapter by chapter, briefly; what you chose yourself; then your questions, numbered, each with its options
and your recommendation.
</reply>

The plan's sections, in this order: Title, Logline, Premise, Genre, tone and audience, Length (total words,
chapters, and where the words go), Strands and curve, Point of view and tense, Voice and style (with how closely
scenes are staged), Main characters (name, role, want, need, arc), Supporting characters, World and setting (and
its rules), Story arc (beginning, middle, end, turning points), Ending, Escalation (the set pieces in order, each
with its first-time step beyond the last), Chapters (proposed: one entry per chapter of their plan, with what
happens in it, what each strand does there and its rough share of the words), Themes and motifs, Rules and
constraints (the author's requirements, in their words).
