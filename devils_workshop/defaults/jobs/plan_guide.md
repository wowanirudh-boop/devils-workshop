Write the writer's guide for this novel: the one document the writer is given with every scene,
beside that scene's brief. The writer sees nothing else, so the guide holds every lasting fact the
writer needs, and nothing that changes as the story goes on.

From the approved story plan, the decisions, the author's preferences and the source notes, write these
sections:
## Voice and style
The narrative voice, point of view and tense, the prose style and rhythm, how dialogue is written, how
much description, the tone, and what to avoid. Concrete enough to write from.

The plan was written for planning; the guide is for a writer who copies its words into every scene. So
translate the plan, never copy its phrasing: where it describes a sound, a voice, a manner or a body with an
adjective or a summary (a "wordless" cry, a "cruel" voice), write instead what goes on the page: the sounds as
they sound, the words said, what the body does.
Write the guide as plain instructions. Where you give an example of what someone says or a sound they make,
give several, different from each other, and call them examples, so the writer varies them and never repeats
one. This holds in every section, the characters' manner and speech included.

Where the approved plan departs from the author's instructions for the writer (a choice made for this book),
the guide follows the plan and says so plainly, as a rule for this book, so the writer knows which to follow.
## Characters
For each character who appears: name, role, age, look, manner, how they speak, their lasting traits and
what drives them. How they are throughout, not what happens to them.
## World and setting
The period and places, how things work (technology, magic, society) and the rules that never bend, and
the recurring sensory details.
## Themes and motifs
What the book is about underneath, and the images and motifs to weave in.
## Rules
The author's requirements and limits on how the book is written (content, vocabulary, what is never
written, what every scene must have), and anything the author said about content, in their words. Not the
changes they asked for to the story itself (a new chapter, a renamed character, an event added or cut): the
plan has carried those, the briefs give them where they happen, and the writer never sees the plan. Describe
any scale, ladder or rule by what each step is like, never by numbered or named labels ("level one", "stage
two") the writer might carry into the prose.

Leave out the plot: no arc, events, twists, secrets or ending, and nothing that happens later in the
book. Anything that only becomes true after an event, a revelation, an experience or a decision (a
changed relationship, a secret someone learns, an outcome, how a character ends up) is not lasting:
leave it out, even from a character's entry. A recurring motif may be described, but never its later
use or payoff (not what a thing will become or how it ends). A mystery's answer is a secret: describe what
is seen, never its explanation. Each scene's brief says what happens and how things stand at that point, so the writer
can never run ahead or give anything away. Write it as instructions to the writer, concrete and complete but not
padded: about 1,000 to 2,000 words. Reply with the guide in Markdown only.
