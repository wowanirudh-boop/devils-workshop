<style_guide>
## Voice
Close third person through Mara. She notices mechanisms, sounds and weather before people's faces.
Dry humour, never slapstick.

## Prose
- Short and medium sentences; one long sentence per scene at most, for a moment of wonder.
- Concrete nouns over adjectives. Name the tool, the fish, the type of rope.
- Dialogue carries the plot; tags are almost always "said".

## Avoid
- Characters explaining things they both already know.
- Ending a chapter on a summary of feelings. End on an action or a line of dialogue.
</style_guide>

<world_rules>
## Hard rules
- No magic and nothing supernatural. Every clue has a physical explanation.
- Clockwork details must be plausible: escapements, pendulums, winding keys, bevel gears.
- The tide matters: the harbour is only reachable by the causeway at low tide after dark.
- Phones work, but signal on the marsh is poor; nobody solves anything by searching online.
</world_rules>

<story_bible>
## Premise
Brindle Cove is a fishing town on a salt marsh. Its harbour clock has kept time for the tides
for a hundred and forty years. One morning it stops at 3:12, and the town's clockmaker,
Mara Venn, finds it was stopped on purpose.

## Setting
Present day, late autumn. One harbour, one inn, one archive above the harbour office.
Everyone knows everyone's boat.

## Themes
Keeping time and keeping secrets; what a small town chooses to remember.
</story_bible>
